01 / ABOUT

About me

I am a laboratory scientist with a background in pharmaceutical biotechnology and cellular neuroscience.

After my MSc at the University of Padova, I moved to Germany to pursue a PhD at the University of Tübingen. My work has taken me from neuron-glia models for pain research to protein-interaction studies in Parkinson’s biology.

I enjoy hands-on laboratory work: developing assays, troubleshooting experiments and making workflows reproducible. I’m interested in bringing this experience to laboratory and R&D roles in biotechnology and pharmaceutical research.

02 / PROJECTS

Projects

Cellular models and protein-interaction studies

01

DZNE · 2023-2025

Mapping LRRK2
interaction networks

Investigating the protein interactions of LRRK2, a protein linked to Parkinson’s disease.

I developed and validated quantitative interaction assays using split-miniTurbo proximity labeling and genetic knockdown, with attention to experimental controls, reproducibility and structured documentation.

  • Proximity labeling
  • Genetic knockdown
  • Protein interactions

DZNE · 2021-2023

Characterizing
LRRK2 inhibitors

Studying candidate inhibitors and the LRRK2 interactome in mammalian cell models.

I carried out and optimized biochemical and cell-based assays, tested LRRK2 inhibitors in vitro and used miniTurbo proximity labeling to study protein interactions.

  • Inhibitor studies
  • Quantitative analysis

NMI · 2017-2020 / PhD

Neuron-glia models

Exploring how sensory neurons and glial cells interact in pain signaling.

Within the European NGN-PET project, I developed primary-cell co-culture assays on microfluidic and microelectrode-array platforms, recorded neuronal activity and analyzed responses.
